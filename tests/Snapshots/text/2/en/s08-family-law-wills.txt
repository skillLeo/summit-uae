Family Law, Wills and Estates in the UAE
Family decisions require clarity about rights and the remedies sought, with care for their effects on children, housing, financial support and assets. We review the circumstances, documents and applicable law, and help you choose practical steps suited to the matter, without assuming that one set of rules governs every family.

Practical Situations We Review with You
Divorce, Custody and Maintenance
We review marriage documents, the parties’ nationalities, religions and residence, and any earlier judgments or agreements to identify the legal framework and competent authority. No single rule applies to all families living in the UAE.
For maintenance, we relate claims to housing, education, medical and other needs, supported by income and expenditure documents. For custody, child contact and travel, we consider the children’s best interests, documents and existing orders, and help draft practical arrangements that can be implemented.
Preparing a Will and Choosing a Registration Route
We identify the assets, beneficiaries, executor and any arrangements for minors, then assess the appropriate registration authority and its requirements. Routes available through local authorities and the DIFC Courts differ in eligibility, procedure and the scope of the document.
A will allows wishes to be recorded within the legal framework governing it. It does not, on its own, replace estate administration procedures or resolve every issue involving jointly owned property, third-party rights or assets in another country.
Dying Without a Will
The absence of a will does not mean that Islamic inheritance rules automatically apply to every non-Muslim. Civil legislation and rules for identifying the applicable law exist, and the outcome depends on the circumstances.
We help gather death and family documents, information about assets and debts, and existing wills, and identify the procedures for establishing the heirs and administering the estate. Any distribution must be based on the applicable rules and decisions of the competent authority, rather than the family’s assumptions alone.
In cross-border matters, we review residence, nationality, the location of assets, foreign judgments or wills, and the requirements for recognising or using them. A foreign document should not be assumed to take effect in the UAE automatically, nor should a local arrangement be assumed to resolve rights in another country.
How We Handle Your Matter
Identify the applicable law: nationality, emirate and types of assets.
Review documents relating to marriage, divorce, assets, accounts and children.
Identify the available options in light of the matter and circumstances.
Develop a course of action that balances the interests of the children and the parties.
Follow up on procedures before the competent authorities where needed.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
Marriage, identity, nationality and residence documents, and children’s birth certificates where relevant.
Earlier judgments and agreements, custody, child contact or travel orders, and any current notices.
Documents relating to income, housing, education, medical care, spending and obligations relevant to the claims.
For estates or wills: details of assets, debts and beneficiaries, a death certificate if available, and existing wills made in the UAE or abroad.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Do not postpone making a will if you have assets or children in the UAE
Gather marriage, identity and asset documents before a dispute arises
Do not sign a family settlement without understanding which rights you are giving up
Check whether your foreign will can be given effect in the UAE
Mistakes to Avoid
Waiting until after a significant change in the family or assets to put a will in place.
Assuming that a will prepared outside the UAE automatically covers assets here
Acting emotionally in family disputes in a way that harms your legal position
Withholding financial documents from your adviser, which undermines the quality of the advice

FAQs
Do Islamic Inheritance Rules Automatically Apply to Every Non-Muslim?
That generalisation is not correct. Civil frameworks and rules for identifying the applicable law exist. Nationality, religion, residence, assets, wills and any permitted choice of law affect the assessment.
How Do I Choose Where to Register a Will?
We review eligibility, assets, beneficiaries, arrangements for minors and earlier wills. Options include the competent local authorities and the DIFC Courts Wills Service, subject to each authority’s requirements. No single route suits everyone.
How Is the Applicable Law Determined in a Non-Muslim Divorce?
Nationality, religion, residence, the relationship, claims, permitted choices and the competent authority must be reviewed. Neither the law of nationality nor a particular civil law should be assumed to apply in every case.
What Happens If Someone Dies Without a Will?
The heirs, assets, liabilities and applicable law must be established before distribution. The ability to deal with certain assets may be temporarily affected, but the absence of a will does not mean that there is no legal framework for inheritance.
Is a Will Prepared Outside the UAE Sufficient?
Its terms, governing law, scope of assets and the method of recognition or use must be reviewed, together with any required authentication and translation. Do not assume that it automatically covers every asset or local arrangement.
Can a Registered Will Be Amended?
Amendment or replacement can be considered under the registration authority’s requirements and procedures. Review the effect on other wills, and do not simply amend a personal copy while the registered document remains different.
