We review the facts, evidence and chronology, identify urgent issues and define the legal assistance appropriate to the matter.

Practical Situations We Review with You
The Matters We Review
Our support covers fraud, breach of trust, forgery, financial crime and cybercrime, incidents involving cheques and virtual assets, and the review of judgments and the procedural options available in relation to them.
Where a dispute arises from a commercial transaction, we examine why money or goods were handed over, the contractual obligations and how the money or goods were dealt with. The other party’s description does not determine the legal characterisation of events. Equally, the existence of a contract does not, by itself, rule out criminal liability.
What Can Change the Assessment of a Defence
A person’s name may appear in a document, or money may reach their account, without the remaining elements of the allegation being established. We examine their actual role, what they knew, their relationship with the other parties and the flow of funds. Each element must be considered in the context of the entire matter.
Good faith needs an explanation supported by the facts, but this does not shift the burden of proving the charge from the prosecution to the accused. The presumption of innocence and the elements of the offence remain central considerations when preparing the defence.
Scope of Work on Your Matter
We analyse the complaint and available documents, organise the facts, review the legal issues and inconsistencies, and prepare the necessary legal materials within the agreed engagement. Where the matter requires advocacy or representation before the police, prosecution or court by an advocate, we coordinate this with a licensed advocate under the applicable rules.
If There Is an Earlier Judgment
Send a copy of the judgment and any notice or document showing when you learned of it or were served, and state whether it has already been challenged. We first identify the stage, deadlines and possible review procedure. New evidence does not automatically make it possible to reopen the case or set aside the judgment.
Do not alter evidence or contact witnesses to coordinate their accounts. Seek help in understanding any statement or document before signing it, while complying with legal deadlines and instructions.
How We Handle Your Matter
Understand the nature of the complaint or summons and the authority that issued it.
Gather documents and evidence before giving any response that has not been properly considered.
Establish the chronology and distinguish each person’s role from that of the other parties.
Analyse criminal intent: was there knowledge, participation or benefit?
Identify the appropriate course: attendance, defence, a challenge to a decision, settlement of certain civil aspects or coordination with an advocate experienced in the relevant area.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
The summons or complaint reference number, the relevant authority and any notice of a date for attendance or a response.
Documents, contracts, transfers and complete conversations relating to the events.
A chronology showing what each party did and what the client knew at the time of the relevant action.
Judgments, service notices and earlier challenges when seeking a judgment review, together with any relevant existing power of attorney.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Do not ignore a summons or postpone attendance without a clear reason.
Do not delete messages, transfer records or digital documents.
Write down a chronology immediately, before you forget the details.
Do not contact the complainant in a way that could be used against you.
Seek legal review before signing documents or making important statements.
Mistakes to Avoid
Assuming that good faith alone is enough without documents to support it.
Speaking emotionally with the complainant or witnesses.
Withholding documents that could help if explained in their proper context.
Treating the matter as minor because it began with a message or a cheque.
Waiting after a judgment is issued until procedural deadlines are close to expiring.

FAQs
What Should I Do If I Receive a Summons from the Police or Prosecution?
Verify its authenticity and the date through official channels, and follow the instructions. Preserve the evidence, prepare a chronology and seek appropriate help without delaying attendance or destroying any document.
Does My Name Appearing in a Document Prove My Involvement in a Crime?
That alone does not establish the elements of the allegation. The document’s authenticity and context, each person’s role and knowledge, and the rest of the evidence must be reviewed. At the same time, the document should not be ignored or denied before it has been examined.
Can an Earlier Criminal Judgment Be Reviewed?
The available procedure depends on the type and level of judgment, the relevant deadlines, its reasoning and the steps already taken. A new document or a fresh reading of the file does not automatically create a right to appeal or seek a retrial.
Are All Cheque Cases Criminal?
No. A claim or enforcement based on a cheque is distinct from liability for conduct criminalised by law. The reason for non-payment and the conduct attributed to the parties must be examined; non-payment alone should not be treated as proof of a crime.
Can WhatsApp Messages Be Used as Evidence?
They may be significant, depending on their authenticity, attribution, completeness, context and how they are submitted. Keep the original conversation and attachments. Do not rely solely on excerpts that omit replies or change the meaning.
Does a Financial Settlement End Criminal Proceedings?
Not in every case. Its effect depends on the charge, the stage of proceedings and the applicable rules. The approach to communication and settlement must be assessed, without pressure, threats or requests to change statements.
