A digital transaction may appear complete while important questions remain unanswered: who paid, who received the currency and what was agreed before the transfer? We connect platform records, wallet records, bank transfers and correspondence to assess a claim or defence on a verifiable basis.

Practical Situations We Review with You
You Sold USDT and Then Faced a Complaint
The dispute may involve a payment from a third party or the use of a genuine seller’s details in a fraud scheme. We review the identity of the trading order holder, the holder of the account that made the payment, delivery instructions, the wallet address and any communications with intermediaries.
Delivery of the currency is an important element of the defence, but it does not resolve the matter on its own. Knowledge of the nature of the transaction, the source of funds, the client’s role and any regulatory obligations connected with the activity must also be examined.
An Investment Loss or a Platform Refusing Withdrawals
We review the investment promises, the operator’s identity, the platform’s terms and the deposit and withdrawal history. We distinguish a contractual dispute from a trading loss and from circumstances that may support a fraud complaint. We then assess which authorities have jurisdiction and whether the responsible party or assets available for enforcement can be reached.
Tracing a transaction does not guarantee recovery of the money. Depending on the information available, a technical expert may be needed to analyse the transactions, or a platform may need to be approached through the appropriate legal procedure.
A Frozen Bank Account or a Transfer Under Enquiry
We review the bank’s or relevant authority’s request and prepare a documented explanation of the source of funds, the purpose of the transaction and its connection to the trading activity. The reason for the restriction and the authority that imposed it determine the response. Providing a trading history alone should not be assumed to result in the freeze being lifted.
The Regulatory Framework
Regulation varies by activity and location. VARA regulates virtual assets in Dubai, excluding the Dubai International Financial Centre, while other jurisdictions have their own frameworks. Trading for your own account is distinct from providing virtual asset services to others.
Do not share your wallet recovery phrase, private keys or verification codes. These are not needed to begin the legal assessment. If you suspect ongoing fraud, promptly notify the bank or platform and the appropriate official channels while preserving the evidence.
How We Handle Your Matter
Start by preserving the available evidence while making the appropriate reports, then connect the digital records with the payment, the agreement and the parties’ identities.
Preserve digital evidence immediately: transaction references, wallet addresses, platform data and all conversations.
Build a complete chronology from the first contact to the last transfer, identifying every party involved.
Carry out a technical analysis of the transfer trail: who sent, who received, when and how the parties are connected.
Assess the legal position: is the person a legitimate seller, a victim or a party to the dispute?
Choose the appropriate route: a complaint, criminal defence, a civil claim or follow-up with a regulator.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
Trading order numbers, platform account details and the downloadable account history.
The on-chain transaction ID (TxID), network name, wallet addresses and timestamps.
Proof of each bank payment, linked to the trade and to lawfully available information identifying the account holder.
Conversations, platform links, investment promises, withdrawal requests and messages from the bank or relevant authorities.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Save transaction references and wallet addresses, and notify the appropriate authorities while preserving the remaining evidence.
Do not send additional money in response to any promise of recovery; this is a second fraud scheme.
Do not delete platform accounts or conversations, even if they are embarrassing.
Do not make public allegations before seeking advice, as this may create additional legal risks.
Seek a legal and technical assessment before filing a complaint or responding to a summons.
Mistakes to Avoid
Relying only on screenshots without verifiable transfer data.
Making further payments to an untrustworthy party promising to recover the money.
Delaying preservation of digital evidence until it becomes difficult to retrieve.
Confusing a legitimate currency sale with participation in fraud.

FAQs
Can Money Lost Through a Cryptocurrency Platform Be Recovered?
The options depend on the operator’s identity, the transfer trail, jurisdiction, available assets and the cooperation obtainable from platforms. Being able to trace a transaction is not the same as being able to recover the funds. An outcome cannot properly be guaranteed before the matter is reviewed.
I Sold USDT and Then Faced a Complaint. Am I Liable?
Delivery of the currency is important to the assessment, but it does not, by itself, determine the remaining elements of liability. The counterparty’s and payer’s identities, source of funds, promises made, your knowledge and role, and any regulatory obligations relating to the activity must be reviewed.
Are Digital Currency Transactions Permitted in the UAE?
The position varies by activity, location, the capacity in which you act and the licence required. Trading for your own account is distinct from providing services to others, managing funds or promotion. The regulatory framework that actually applies to the transaction must be identified.
Are Screenshots Enough to Prove a Transfer?
They may help, but it is preferable to preserve the original records, network data, complete conversations and proof of payment. The movement of the asset must be linked to the parties’ identities and their agreement; an on-chain record alone does not explain why a transfer was made.
Where Should I Start If I Discover Digital Fraud?
Stop making further payments, notify the appropriate authorities and preserve evidence at the same time. Do not pay new fees to an unknown party promising recovery, and do not give anyone access to your wallet.
Does Providing a Trading History Automatically Unfreeze an Account?
No. The source and reason for the restriction must be established, including whether it concerns a bank compliance review or a measure imposed by an investigating authority or court. The documents and response are prepared according to that reason.
