Practical Situations We Review with You
Legal Review and Certified Translation
Legal review explains terms and obligations and compares their effect across different versions. Where certified translation is required for official use, we coordinate with a legal translator registered with the competent authority for the required language.
We establish at the outset whether you need to understand a document, review a bilingual contract, obtain an official translation or have a document issued abroad attested. These are different services, and a matter may require more than one of them.
Is Attestation of the Translation Enough?
Attestation of a document or signature is different from certification of a translation and the accuracy of its content. The original document may also require attestation, depending on where it was issued and how it will be used. We explain the steps required for the specific transaction without assuming that one procedure covers them all.
Documents include commercial and investment contracts, shareholders’ agreements, powers of attorney, judgments and written submissions, as well as incorporation documents, resolutions, authorisations and relevant personal documents. We check names, numbers, amounts, dates, definitions, annexes and the clause specifying which language prevails.
How We Handle Your Matter
Establish the document’s purpose, the authority to which it will be submitted and the languages required.
Read the original and compare the versions, definitions, annexes and key details.
Identify differences in meaning or legal effect and any certification or attestation requirements.
Explain the scope of work, timeframe and fees, and coordinate with the appropriate specialist.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
A complete, legible copy of the original, including annexes, stamps and relevant pages.
Any previous translation and, for multilingual contracts, the clause specifying which language prevails in the event of a discrepancy.
The required language, purpose, receiving authority and deadline for submission or signature.
Supporting information and reference documents confirming the spelling of names, numbers, capacities and dates, where needed.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Identify the authority to which the translation will be submitted before work begins; requirements differ.
Always check names, numbers and dates after translation.
Check the authority’s requirements and do not rely on machine translation alone in place of the required professional certification.
Request a legal review of important contracts, as well as a linguistic translation.
Mistakes to Avoid
Relying on machine translation for contracts or official documents
Signing a translated document without understanding what each clause means
Overlooking differences in legal terminology between legal systems
Failing to check numbers and dates, where errors most often occur

FAQs
Can Machine Translation Be Used for Official Submission?
Machine translation alone is not treated as certified legal translation. Check the receiving authority’s requirements, the language and the type of document, and use a registered translator where required. Automated tools may help with an initial understanding, subject to appropriate human review.
What Is the Difference Between Translation Certification and Attestation?
Translation certification concerns compliance with professional translation requirements and those of the receiving authority. Attestation may concern the original document, stamp or signature. A transaction may require more than one procedure.
Is a Translation by Someone Fluent in the Language Sufficient?
It may help you understand the document personally, but it does not replace the certification required for official use or a review of the legal effect of important clauses. The service required depends on the purpose for which the document will be used.
How Long Does a Translation Take?
The timeframe is determined after establishing the number of pages, language, complexity, required certification or attestations, and the specialist’s availability. We do not promise a deadline before reviewing these requirements.
Does Reviewing the Translation Include Reviewing the Contract Itself?
Both can be included in a clearly defined engagement. Comparing versions identifies differences in meaning; reviewing the contract also addresses rights, obligations, risks and the terms that should be amended or negotiated.
