Property and Tenancy Disputes in Dubai and the UAE
A dispute may concern a delayed property handover, an undisclosed defect, a home that cannot be properly used or a deposit that has not been returned after move-out. We identify the obligation in dispute, how it can be proved and the remedy that addresses its consequences, while reviewing both parties’ obligations and the competent authority.

Practical Situations We Review with You
Delayed Handover and Disputes with Developers
We review the sale agreement, completion date, extension clauses, notices, payment plan and project status. We assess how the delay affects a claim for performance, compensation or termination, taking account of the applicable property rules and both parties’ conduct.
A developer announcing a new date does not automatically give the buyer a right to terminate. Equally, an extension clause does not give the developer an unrestricted exemption from its obligations. The legal consequences of suspending payments require a separate assessment.
Maintenance, Defects and Loss of Use
For water leaks, damp and utility problems, documenting the defect, its duration and its effect on use is essential. We review the contract, handover report, notices, responses from the landlord or management and technical reports.
Where the damage affects health or safety, an independent technical report is useful, together with relevant medical reports if there is an actual health impact. Photographs show the visible condition but may not be enough to establish its cause or who is responsible for repairs.
Eviction, Deposits and Handover
We review the grounds for eviction and the form and date of the notice. In Dubai, grounds for eviction during a tenancy differ from those on which a landlord seeks eviction at the end of the term. There is therefore no single notice period that applies to every ground.
When a property is vacated, we organise evidence of handover, the unit’s condition, utility readings, keys, outstanding amounts, cheques and the deposit. Physically leaving the property does not, by itself, establish that obligations have ended or determine the legally recognised handover date.
The Competent Authority
Many tenancy disputes in Dubai are heard by the Rental Disputes Center. Jurisdiction differs for sale and development disputes and for certain areas or special contracts. We identify the competent authority by reference to the property, the relationship and the remedy sought. Any representation requiring a particular professional status is arranged in accordance with that authority’s requirements.
For defects in a sold property, we review the nature of the defect, when it existed, what was disclosed, the buyer’s knowledge, warranties and relevant time limits. In a tenancy, we distinguish the appearance of a defect from responsibility for repairing it and its effect on use. Establishing the cause may require an independent technical report.
How We Handle Your Matter
Read the contract carefully: the handover date, delay clauses, compensation arrangements and termination terms.
Review both parties’ obligations: have you made all payments, and has the developer failed to meet its obligations?
Gather formal and informal correspondence with the developer or other party.
Assess the options: termination, compensation, settlement or proceedings before the competent authority.
Choose the most suitable course based on the strength of the case and the time available.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
The sale or tenancy agreement and its schedules, Ejari if available, the payment plan and receipts.
Correspondence, notices, acceptance and handover reports, and any previous settlement or complaint.
Photographs of defects, the dates they appeared, repair requests, and relevant technical or medical reports where there has been an actual impact.
Judgments or enforcement files, cheques, deposit details and disputed amounts, if any.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Keep all correspondence with the developer, broker or landlord
Do not stop paying without understanding how this affects your position under the contract
Document defects with photographs and independent reports as soon as you discover them
Do not sign a settlement with the developer without understanding which rights you are giving up
Mistakes to Avoid
Relying on oral promises from a salesperson that were not included in the contract
Abruptly stopping payments without reviewing the effect on your legal position
Overlooking force majeure or extension clauses in the contract
Signing a settlement under pressure or out of exhaustion without reviewing it
Check a Dubai rent increase

FAQs
Does a Developer’s Delay Automatically Entitle Me to Terminate?
The answer cannot be determined by the length of the delay alone. The sale agreement, extensions, notices, project status and both parties’ obligations must be reviewed. Suspending payments also requires a separate assessment to avoid creating a breach on the buyer’s part.
What Should I Do If I Discover a Defect After Handover?
Document the condition, the date and any earlier disclosures, and notify the responsible party in a way that can be proved. A technical report may be needed to establish the cause, how long the defect has existed and its effect, while taking account of safety, necessary repairs and preservation of evidence.
How Do I Start a Complaint Against a Developer in Dubai?
Gather the contract, payment records, correspondence and project information, and identify the remedy you seek. The appropriate regulatory or judicial route should be reviewed. Filing a regulatory complaint does not automatically replace a claim for payment or termination.
Can a Landlord Evict Without Notice?
The grounds, notices and procedures vary according to the applicable rules and the remedy sought. In Dubai, certain grounds for eviction during the tenancy differ from those connected with its expiry, so no single notice period applies to all grounds.
Does Moving Out End My Obligations?
Physically leaving does not, by itself, determine the handover date or end your obligations. Document the keys, the unit’s condition, utilities, amounts, cheques and deposit, and review the contract and any agreement or decision of the competent authority.
