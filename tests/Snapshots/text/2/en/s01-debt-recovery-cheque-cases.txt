Practical Situations We Review with You
Establishing the Basis of the Debt and the Appropriate Procedure
We review the contract, invoices, delivery records, previous payments and correspondence. The assessment also covers the debtor’s legal identity, the due date, any objections to the goods or services, and information about assets obtained through lawful means.
An established right to payment is different from the practical ability to recover it. We therefore assess the claim separately from the prospects of enforcement, particularly where the debtor has left the country or their business is in financial difficulty.
Dishonoured Cheques and Enforcement
Under the Commercial Transactions Law, a cheque on which the bank has recorded non-payment because funds are absent or insufficient constitutes an enforceable instrument. It may be possible to apply for enforcement without first bringing a substantive claim to establish the debt, subject to the cheque’s particulars, enforcement requirements and available defences.
Not every dishonoured cheque involves a crime. We examine the reason for non-payment and the parties’ conduct to determine whether there is a separate criminal aspect, rather than assuming that non-payment alone is sufficient for a criminal complaint.
How We Assist Creditors and Debtors
For creditors: checking the amount claimed, preparing a formal demand, negotiating a settlement and preparing the claim or enforcement file.
For debtors: reviewing the basis of the debt, payments, set-off and objections, and considering a repayment agreement that takes existing obligations into account.
For both parties: drafting a settlement that specifies the amount, instalments, security, consequences of default and return of documents once payment is complete.
How We Handle Your Matter
Identify the basis of the debt: a contract, invoice, cheque, transfer or written acknowledgement.
Arrange the documents chronologically to establish the relationship, the debt and when payment became due.
Assess the strengths and weaknesses of the claim, particularly where there is no detailed contract.
Consider settlement or a formal claim before taking a more costly step.
Choose the appropriate route: negotiation, a formal demand, a civil claim, enforcement or criminal proceedings where the necessary conditions are met.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
The contract or accepted quotation, purchase orders and details of the contracting party.
Invoices, delivery records or acceptance of completed work, a payment statement and the outstanding balance claimed.
The original cheque and the bank’s confirmation of the reason for non-payment, with a clear copy sent for the initial review.
Correspondence, acknowledgements of debt, promises to pay, and any earlier settlements or judgments.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Keep the original cheque or notice of non-payment, if available.
Collect invoices, contracts, purchase orders and delivery records.
Preserve WhatsApp messages and emails containing an acknowledgement of the debt or promises to pay.
Do not accept an oral repayment plan; request a written, signed schedule.
Do not delay pursuing payment, as documents may be lost or the debtor may become harder to reach.
Mistakes to Avoid
Handing over the original cheque or documents without a written agreement.
Relying on repeated oral promises.
Sending threatening messages that could later weaken your position.
Failing to document the delivery of goods or services.
Confusing commercial pressure with the appropriate legal procedure.

FAQs
Is a Dishonoured Cheque Sufficient to Start Enforcement?
A cheque on which the bank has recorded non-payment because funds are absent or insufficient may constitute an enforceable instrument under Article 667 of the Commercial Transactions Law. Its particulars, the bank’s confirmation and the requirements for enforcement must be reviewed. Documents concerning the underlying relationship remain important when assessing any defence or challenge.
Can a Debt Be Recovered Without a Written Contract?
Invoices, correspondence, purchase orders, delivery or transfer records and acknowledgements of debt may help establish the claim. However, an invoice issued by one party alone does not conclusively establish acceptance of the obligation, the amount or the payment due date.
Should I Start with Settlement or Formal Action?
We weigh the strength of the evidence, the debtor’s conduct, the amount owed, information about assets and the risks of delay. Where the circumstances allow, negotiation may proceed alongside action to preserve your rights.
What If the Debtor Has Left the UAE?
We assess jurisdiction, the location of assets, the debtor’s legal identity and the prospects of enforcement. Leaving the country does not, by itself, make a claim impossible, but it may affect the cost and how the matter is pursued.
Can the Debt Be Repaid in Instalments?
A repayment agreement can specify the outstanding balance, instalments, due dates, security, consequences of default and what happens to the cheques and proceedings. Before handing over any original document, check the signatory’s authority and the agreement’s effect on existing rights.
