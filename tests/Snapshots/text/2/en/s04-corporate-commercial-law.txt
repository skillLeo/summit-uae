Practical Situations We Review with You
Contract Drafting and Review
We review the subject of the agreement, obligations, payment, delivery, acceptance, warranties, limits of liability, termination and dispute resolution. We explain the practical effect of the terms and propose amendments that can be negotiated, proportionate to the transaction’s value and risks.
Our work covers services, supply and distribution agreements, confidentiality and cooperation agreements, and contracts relating to technology projects and products. The scope of review is determined by the nature of the business and the agreed governing law and jurisdiction.
Partnerships and Rights in Projects
A promise to grant a share in a future company is different from the actual registration and transfer of that share. Likewise, allowing someone to use a project or its source code does not, on its own, establish that the rights have been transferred. We review agreements, correspondence, incorporation documents and the limits of the permission to use the project.
When structuring a new partnership, we address cash and non-cash contributions, intellectual property rights, management, profits, exit arrangements and situations in which the venture runs into difficulty. If a dispute has already arisen, we identify the rights supported by documents and the options available to preserve them.
Disputes with Partners, Customers and Suppliers
We compare the contract terms with what was actually performed and review invoices, approvals, amendments and correspondence. We then assess the parties’ respective claims, the risks of terminating the contract or suspending performance, and the merits of negotiation or legal action.
If the dispute requires accounting or technical expertise, we define the questions the expert needs to examine rather than commissioning a general review that does not address the issue in dispute.
For commercial agencies, distribution, restructuring or mergers, we identify the nature of the arrangement, any specific legal regime that may govern it, the approvals required and the rights affected. Standard services agreement terms are no substitute for a review of these specialist matters.
How We Handle Your Matter
Review the existing structure: incorporation documents, the partnership agreement and documented authority.
Identify the point of disagreement: a written term, an undocumented obligation or a disputed decision?
Assess each party’s legal position and identify its strengths and weaknesses.
Evaluate the options: negotiation, a formal notice, restructuring or legal action.
Establish a course of action to protect interests before the situation escalates.
Documents That Help Us Get Started
The contract and its schedules, quotations, approvals, amendments and material correspondence.
Incorporation documents, the licence, ownership register, partnership agreement, authorisations and relevant resolutions.
Invoices, payments, proof of delivery or acceptance of work, and a record of objections.
Evidence of ownership of the product, brand or project and the limits of permission to use it, together with any promise of shares or investment.
Send clear, complete copies initially and retain the originals. Do not send passwords, verification codes or financial account access credentials. An appropriate method for sharing sensitive documents can be agreed.
Before Taking the Next Step
Useful First Steps
Do not sign a partnership agreement without clearly defining authority and exit arrangements
Record every amendment or side agreement in writing, even between friends
Keep company and personal accounts separate from day one
Review commercial contracts before a dispute arises
Mistakes to Avoid
Relying on personal trust instead of a written agreement
Using a generic contract template that does not suit the business or partnership
Overlooking jurisdiction and arbitration clauses until you need to rely on them
Failing to agree exit arrangements in advance

FAQs
Can a Company Be Wholly Foreign-Owned?
This is possible for many activities and structures, but the activity, licensing authority, legal form, approvals and any specific restrictions must be checked. Permission to own a company does not, by itself, authorise every business activity.
What Is the Difference Between a Free Zone and the Mainland?
The licensing authority, operating conditions, market access, approvals and certain obligations differ. The choice should reflect the business activity, customers, place of operation and future plans, rather than fees alone or an assumption of a general tax exemption.
What Should I Do If I Disagree with My Business Partner?
Gather the partnership agreement, ownership and authority documents, payment records, resolutions and correspondence. The rights supported by the evidence and the options for settlement, separation or formal action should then be reviewed before taking any step that could affect the company’s assets or operations.
Is an Oral Agreement Binding?
It may create an obligation in certain circumstances. However, how it can be proved and whether the law requires a particular form both need to be reviewed. Record obligations, amendments and approvals in a form appropriate to the transaction.
Does a Promise of a Share in a Future Company Make Me a Registered Partner?
A contractual promise is distinct from the actual registration and transfer of a share. Correspondence, contributions, incorporation documents and the terms of the promise must be reviewed. Permission to use a project or its source code should not be assumed to transfer ownership automatically.
